The test

Nine plates our model read correctly and the standard open-source reader did not. Cover the answer and try first — that is the whole demonstration.

Where it fails

Thirty-four of the eighty-three were wrong. Almost all of them by a single character, and almost always a stroke that survives at 200 pixels and does not at 60.

This is the honest limit. The plates here are 45–130 pixels wide. Commercial ANPR cameras are specified at 100–300 pixels because ten millimetres of stroke needs two pixels to survive. Below that, 8 and B become the same pixels — for us, for any model, and for you.

Measured against what

EasyOCR was given every advantage our model gets: the same frames, the same multi-frame voting, and the same Indian plate-grammar correction applied to its output. A baseline denied the post-processing would not survive the first question about it.

How the number was produced

Split by vehicle, not by frame

  • One vehicle contributes ~11 crops of the same plate
  • Splitting frame-wise puts a plate on both sides and scores memorisation
  • We caught exactly that in an earlier detector: 89% of its validation vehicles were also in training, and its 0.995 mAP was meaningless

Checkpoint chosen on validation

  • A third set, never scored on
  • Choosing the best epoch on test reports the maximum of ~18 noisy draws as if it were one
  • That mistake alone moves this model from 59% to 82%

Two metrics, kept apart

  • Exact match — all ten characters right
  • Character accuracy — per character
  • They differ by 34 points here; quoting the second as the first is the most common way a 95% claim is made
